ELE5001 · Telecommunication Design

Planning an LTE-Advanced radio access network.

I used CelPlanner to plan and evaluate a small LTE-Advanced radio access network, balancing radio coverage, interference, user data rate, traffic demand and site count across an assigned service area.

Project boundary This is university simulation and planning work, not a live mobile-network deployment. The evidence is cropped from my authored report; raw assessment PDFs and personal identifiers are not published.

01 · Challenge

Balance competing radio objectives

The design needed to cover an 18.5 × 15.5 km area while limiting interference, supporting offered traffic and avoiding unnecessary base-station sites.

02 · Decision

Use 18 tri-sector sites in Band 8

I modelled 18 eNodeBs with three cells per site at 0.9 GHz, then allocated Group 3 channels 37–54 in a three-sector reuse pattern.

03 · Implementation

Tune placement, antennas and power

Topography, morphology and population informed site placement. I adjusted positions, azimuth, downtilt and transmit power to close coverage gaps and reduce sector overlap.

04 · Evidence

Compare coverage, C/I and traffic

The simulation reported more than 91% downlink area above −90 dBm, 87% uplink above −93 dBm and C/I above 20 dB across 90% of the service area.

05 · Learning

Coverage is not capacity

Traffic analysis exposed high session rejection under heavy demand. The design met its core RF objectives, but 18 sites did not provide sufficient capacity for every simulated load.

Coverage Downlink >91% · Uplink 87%

Area above the project thresholds of −90 dBm downstream and −93 dBm upstream.

Interference C/I >20 dB across 90%

Three-sector frequency reuse and antenna tuning reduced co-channel overlap.

Mobility 30–40% lower maximum rate

Simulated users at 120 km/h achieved lower maximum data rate than static users.

Recommended next steps Capacity-focused optimisation

The report recommends load balancing, cell-reselection and power-control tuning; these improvements were proposed, not implemented.

CSE5INE(BU-1) · Intermediate Network Engineering

From addressing plans to multi-protocol routing.

01 · Challenge

Translate topology into reachability

Each lab required converting addressing and routing requirements into a working network, then separating physical, interface and protocol faults when traffic failed.

02 · Decision

Verify in layers

I adopted a consistent troubleshooting order: physical connectivity, interface state, protocol operation, routing information and end-to-end traffic.

03 · Implementation

Configure, observe and compare

The sequence covered IOS device and interface configuration, manual routes, RIPv1/RIPv2, single- and multi-area OSPF, DR/BDR behaviour and basic eBGP.

04 · Evidence

Use protocol state to prove the path

Routing tables, neighbour state, protocol status, ping and traceroute were used to verify learned paths and isolate missing advertisements or area errors.

05 · Learning

Troubleshoot the system, not one command

The progression strengthened my ability to connect routing theory with observable behaviour and to diagnose connectivity problems in a repeatable order.
